BTL marketing4 min read

What is BTL marketing, and when does it beat mass media?

Below-the-line (BTL) marketing is any promotion that reaches people in a specific place rather than through a broadcast channel — a product display in a mall atrium, a sampling counter, a tricycle fleet through a residential colony, an LED van on an arterial road. Above-the-line media buys reach; BTL buys presence. For a broader primer on the term itself, One Day Agency's guide to below-the-line marketing is a useful read; what follows is how it plays out on the ground in Hyderabad.

It wins when the brief is local. A launch that needs to be seen in three Hyderabad neighbourhoods, a dealer opening, a festival-week push at one mall — these are jobs where a television spot spends most of its money on people who will never visit. A BTL marketing campaign can be pointed at a 5 km catchment and re-routed halfway through if the first week shows a better street.

It also wins on proof. Every format Equestrian Media operates comes back with dated photographs from the route or the floor, so a marketing manager can see what ran instead of reading an impression estimate. The trade-off is scale: BTL is built to own a market, not a country. Most of our clients pair it with the media that lives in the same environment — DOOH screens, façade hoardings, cinema — so the brand is seen on the way in, at the activation and on the way out.

Brand activation3 min read

Brand activation vs experiential marketing: what is the difference?

The two terms get used as if they were interchangeable, and in most briefs they nearly are. Experiential marketing is the idea — let people experience the brand rather than just see it. A brand activation is the event that does it: the car on a 20 × 20 ft atrium footprint, the two-wheeler line-up, the stage with an emcee, the sampling drive.

The useful distinction is who is accountable for what. An experiential concept can be excellent on paper and still fail on the day because the display arrived late, the promoters were never briefed or the mall had not approved the artwork. That is why we plan a brand activation as production, not as an idea: space confirmation, approvals, set-up, staffing and dismantling are all in the same scope.

If you are choosing an agency, ask two questions. Who holds the venue relationship — do they book the space or sub-let it? And what do you receive afterwards — a summary, or photographs from the floor with dates? At LuLu Mall, Hyderabad we hold the advertising rights directly, so both answers are short.

Mall activation5 min read

How to plan a mall activation at LuLu Mall, Hyderabad.

Start with the footprint, not the creative. The central atrium at LuLu Mall takes display spaces from 8 × 8 ft up to a 20 × 20 ft launch set-up; kiosks in the shopper flow are 4 × 4 ft or 6 × 6 ft with a 3 × 6 ft backdrop. A vehicle display, a stage with sound and a sampling counter each need a different one, and availability is confirmed at booking — so fix the dates and the footprint first, then design into it.

Second, plan the walk. The same shopper passes the façade hoardings on the approach road, the main atrium LED as they enter, the activation itself, and backlit boards at every lift lobby on the way out. A mall activation that uses two or three of those touchpoints costs more than the atrium alone but is remembered as one campaign rather than one stall.

Third, leave time for approvals. Artwork is approved by mall management, façade installation runs between 11 PM and 6 AM, and promoters need a brief before the doors open. Share your dates early and we will tell you what is achievable — the footfall (25,000+ on an average day, more at weekends and festivals) will be there either way.

Outdoor advertising4 min read

Billboard advertising in Hyderabad, format by format.

In India the words billboard and hoarding mean the same thing: a large fixed panel, front-lit or backlit, on a building, gantry or roadside structure. What matters more than the name is where it sits in the audience's day. A roadside hoarding is seen at speed; a mall façade hoarding is seen by people who slow down, park and walk in.

Pole kiosks work differently. Individually they are small; in a run of twenty along an arterial road they deliver frequency — the same message every hundred metres. Digital wall posters and no-parking boards do the same job inside a residential catchment, and round flex on dividers faces traffic in both directions. We plan outdoor advertising by route so these formats reinforce each other instead of competing.

The parts that slow outdoor campaigns down are rarely creative. Municipal permissions, printing lead times, night installation windows and maintenance decide whether a campaign starts on the date it was sold. Ask any outdoor agency who handles each of those — and whether you will get dated photographs of every site as proof of display.

Mobile media4 min read

LED van, Tata Ace or auto branding: which fits the brief?

Mobile media is the fastest way to put a brand on a street that has no hoarding. The choice between formats comes down to three things: how much of the city you need to cover, whether the message moves (video) or stays still, and how long the campaign runs.

An LED van carries full-motion video and runs a 70 km route over 8 hours a day — the format for launches and anything that needs sound and movement. A Tata Ace covers the same route with static branding at a fraction of the monthly rate, which makes it the workhorse for real-estate and retail campaigns that run for weeks. Auto-top and sticker branding is bought by the fleet — 500 autos minimum, concentrated in one area — and is the cheapest way to saturate a locality for a month.

For a market-level brief, a tricycle or look-walker team covering about 5 km a day, at roughly ₹1,500 a day, is often enough to test an area before a larger spend. Indicative rates for all of these are on our rate card; current pricing depends on route, duration and artwork.

DOOH3 min read

How a DOOH loop works: spots, slots and rotations.

Digital out-of-home is sold in loops, and the arithmetic is worth understanding before you buy. On the main atrium LED at LuLu Mall, a spot is 20 seconds long and sits in a nine-slot loop, so the loop runs every three minutes. Across the mall's 14 operating hours that is roughly 280 plays of your spot a day — every one of them in front of shoppers walking into the building.

Floor-level LED towers on the first, second and third floors work the same way with 10-second spots, which brings the count to around 780 plays a day per tower. The screens are vertical (360 × 960), so creative built for a landscape television spot needs to be re-cut rather than squeezed.

Two practical notes. First, a DOOH spot has no audio, so the message has to land visually in the first two seconds. Second, DOOH is strongest as the second touchpoint: a shopper who has just passed a façade hoarding for the same brand recognises the screen instantly. We plan DOOH alongside façade, backlit and activation space for exactly that reason.

Cinema advertising3 min read

Cinema advertising: what a captive audience is actually worth.

Every other medium competes with a phone. Cinema is the one environment where the audience has paid to sit still, the lights are down and the screen is the only thing to look at. That is why a 30-second cinema spot is remembered in a way the same spot on a feed is not.

The Cinépolis at LuLu Mall, Hyderabad has five screens and 1,427 seats with Dolby Atmos and RealD 3D, and cinema advertising there comes in three layers: on-screen before the film, foyer branding, and standees where the queue forms. Used together they reach the same person three times in one evening.

Cinema works best for brands whose audience skews to the multiplex — automotive, premium retail, entertainment and food — and for campaigns that already have a mall activation or façade presence in the same building. Start with the dates of a big release and plan backwards from there.
